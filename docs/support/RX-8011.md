# Workload sizing and recovery after N9

This guidance describes the reviewed N9 observations and the current public contracts. It is not a latency SLA or a promise that every collection below a document ceiling fits every operation. Keep your application's authoritative records, and qualify its actual schema, text, queries, selected providers and execution host.

## Updates, queries and restores have different costs

| Operation | What determines the work | Practical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add or replace one record | Admission and analysis of the supplied record, changed postings/statistics and selected transactional providers | A successful small update does not predict the cost of searching or rebuilding the whole collection. N9 establishes no new single-record update latency guarantee. |
| Selective lexical query | Expanded terms and the complete candidate upper bound, followed by evaluation, scoring and page selection | Posting-driven planning can reduce eligible work. The observed selective gains do not apply to every query. |
| Broad exact-total query | Every required candidate evaluation and matching contribution, including requested facets or explanations | A page of 20 hits still has an exact total. A small page, or `limit: 0`, is not an early-stop search. |
| Source export and restore | Exported source, complete encoding/decoding, receiving analysis, index/provider construction and validation | Export, restore and many small adds have distinct budgets. Test the full recovery path, not only ingestion. |

The mutation owner prepares the submitted changes before one publication; its cost is data- and provider-dependent. Search computes complete eligibility and scoring before returning its exact count and page. Browse, negative-only queries and opaque per-record predicates can require broad traversal; adding a filter does not universally make a query cheap. These are current [mutation](../../src/core/mutations/owner.ts), [candidate](../../src/core/query/candidates.ts) and [search](../../src/core/query/search.ts) behaviors, not complexity or timing guarantees.

The [N9 Node collectors](../../bench/n9/paired-child.mjs) time builds, queries, exports and restores, not a calibrated single-record replacement cohort. Earlier replacement timings, including the historical RX-4001 observations, must not be relabeled as current update latency. The [native paired results](../evidence/RX-8009.md) retain both improvements and regressions. Their selected 100-record tails and larger small-sample observations are different evidence; there is no universal 10,000-record query p95.

## Bounds are admission ceilings, not a safe collection size

Current ceilings include 10,000 documents, 256 records in one mutation batch, 100 returned hits, 10 million search work units and 50 million mutation work units. Independent token, dictionary, posting, source, queue and output bounds also apply. Work units are accounting units, not milliseconds. Supported limit overrides can tighten bounds; they do not raise these ceilings. See the actual [limits](../../src/core/limits/limits.ts).

**The ordinary deadline defaults to 30 seconds**, or the lower configured maximum. The maximum permitted operation deadline is 60 seconds; elapsed queue time also counts against the operation deadline. N9 collectors explicitly supplied `deadlineMs: 60000`; those measurements used unchanged default work/admission caps, but did not test the ordinary deadline default. A reference to an N9 “default 60-second deadline” should be read as that explicit maximum-deadline configuration, not the library's default. [Option admission](../../src/core/contracts/options.ts), the [execution owner](../../src/core/execution/engine.ts) and the [restore collector](../../bench/n9/restore-case.mjs) establish this distinction.

Do not increase a claimed supported collection size from the document ceiling, a fast selective query, or successful batched ingestion. Record failures with their operation and typed details. In particular, the [RX-8010 high-cardinality build attempt](../evidence/RX-8010/node/high-cardinality-10000-raveldex-2.json) reached `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` even with an explicit 60-second operation deadline. Its batched build took 77,617 ms overall. The preserved fresh retry succeeded under identical caps; the host-delay cause is unestablished, and the retry does not erase the refusal.

## Observed recovery capacity

These are specific local observations, not general limits for other text, analyzers or providers:

| Frozen workload/configuration | Observed result | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 adversarial records; one `body` text field and `group` metadata; basic-v1 analyzer; selected first-party source/source-phrase or source plus positions/offsets/position-phrase providers | RX-8008 completes all fourteen selected 10,000-record restores across Node direct and headless Chrome direct/real-worker normal and diagnostic cases | These exact source snapshot inputs and selected registrations restore within the unchanged 50-million work cap with an explicit 60-second deadline. This is not “all 10k collections restore.” |
| Real-document and high-cardinality 1,000-record source snapshots in RX-8009 | Restore already succeeds before and after the native optimizations | This is a control, not a new capacity threshold. |
| Real-document and high-cardinality 10,000-record source exports in RX-8009 | `WORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; dependent restores are Not run | No successful restore-capacity claim is available for these cells. Export refusal is not a restore result. |
| Real-document 10,000 broad-prefix and high-cardinality 10,000 source-phrase requests in RX-8009 | Search work refusals remain | Successful restore or selective search does not imply every query succeeds. |

The [RX-8008 records](../evidence/RX-8008.md), [native capacity analysis](../evidence/RX-8009/node-review.md) and [frozen workloads](../../bench/n9/workloads.mjs) preserve the exact inputs, schema, provider details and outcomes. The selected hosts were Node 24.21.0 on Darwin 27.0.0 arm64 / Apple M5 Pro and headless Chrome 156.0.8078.80. Custom or wrapped registrations retain their public callback protocol and may incur different work; the private first-party bulk path is not an application-selectable capability.

## Handle known failure and unknown outcome separately

A successful search has `completeness: 'complete'`; its `total` counts every match. `WORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` or an expansion refusal is a failed request, not a successful partial count. Preserve the typed code/details and give the application a deliberate recovery path. A narrower application query may be appropriate, but do not silently change matching semantics or display zero as though the failed search found nothing.

Ordinary native mutations and source restore prepare private state and publish once. An authoritative validation/work/deadline/cancellation failure before commit preserves the previous root. A series of separately committed add batches is not one atomic whole-collection import. Aborting later work cannot undo batches that already committed.

A lost worker reply or storage timeout can instead leave the outcome unknown. For mutations, retain the captured operation ID and use that surviving worker/session's mutation receipt. Source-restore receipts use the separate worker-snapshot reconciliation API. A pending or expired receipt does not prove rollback; receipt windows are bounded and are not durable deduplication. If the owner/session is gone or the result cannot be established, recover a new owner from authoritative data rather than blindly retrying a write. [Worker and snapshot integration](../api/HOSTS.md#one-owner-module-worker) describes the public APIs; [snapshot publication](../../src/adapters/snapshots/snapshots.ts) preserves the commit boundary.

Cancel obsolete work with `AbortSignal`, handle settlement, and release resources deliberately. Destroy borrowed UI before disposing its engine; await worker-adapter cleanup and release an externally owned endpoint yourself. Moving an engine to a worker changes its execution location, not its exact-total contract or work limits. Transport size, response timeouts and unknown receipts remain separate from native operation budgets.

## Preserve capability and evidence boundaries

Core retains index statistics, not original text or positions. Select source retention for every schema text field when source export is needed, and select the required position/phrase/snippet registrations explicitly. Rebuild under a new owner when changing the analyzer or provider configuration. Native snapshots require an application's exact matching Node-runtime qualification; source rebuilds do not promise cross-environment membership/ranking identity. [Compatibility](../api/COMPATIBILITY.md) remains authoritative.

[RX-8010](../evidence/RX-8010.md) checks selected foreign memberships, not equivalent ranking, output, transactional updates or cancellation. Complete foreign results and native exact-total/top-20 selection do different work. Fuse fuzzy and Pagefind static-site tracks remain separate. These results do not establish an overall engine winner.

Encoded snapshot bytes, bundle gzip and sampled process `heapUsed` are not retained/peak engine heap. N9 provides no universal memory budget. Its browser observations do not establish physical input/paint latency, human screen-reader behavior, native Safari or a broad device/browser performance envelope. Check the target application's actual browser, assistive technology, device, long/broad queries, cancellation, failures and recovery. Existing automated checks do not replace those observations.

N9 completion does not publish a package, choose a public version, release/tag the repository, add capabilities or qualify a new environment. Those remain separate owner decisions.
